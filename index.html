The Power of a Story

Sri Nihal Tammana

Founder of Recycle My Battery

One story. A ten-year-old. A mission to recycle 3 billion batteries.

17 · Junior at Monroe Township High School, New Jersey

02 · The Spark

How one article lit the fuse

On his tenth birthday in 2019, Sri Nihal Tammana read a news article about a lithium-ion battery that exploded in California. Most kids his age were busy with their presents. He could not look away from the devastation: the immense fires and the contamination that followed.

He had always cared. He grew up on climate-change videos, and it broke his heart to watch someone toss plastic into a river. But caring quietly is easy. That article asked for more. For the first time, fixing it felt like his job.

When he looked into it, the scale hit him. Billions of batteries in the trash every year, and more than four in ten people had no idea they were dangerous. He decided to be one of the people who actually did something. A fact can tell you a problem exists. A story is what makes you act on it.

03 · The Problem

Dead batteries don't just disappear

3B batteries tossed in the trash every year
41% of people have no idea they're dangerous
41% in the dark · 59% aware

What a single alkaline battery carries into the ground:

  • Zinc
  • Manganese
  • Nickel
  • Cadmium trace

One battery looks harmless, and the amounts inside really are small. But three billion of them a year stops being small. The metals leach into the soil and the water we drink, slow and quiet, while most of us never think twice about the dead battery rolling around in a drawer. Closing that gap, between the damage and the shrug, is the whole point.

04 · The Struggle

Every founder gets told no

He was ten, and most people heard his age before they heard his idea. He spent July 2019 going door to door, asking neighbors to recycle their dead batteries. Plenty of them shut the door.

The big organizations were not much easier. Call2Recycle, one of the largest battery recycling groups in North America, said no the first time he asked. For a kid with a clipboard and a stack of battery-shaped flyers, it would have been an easy place to stop.

He kept going anyway.

The advice that kept him going His dad told him he couldn't let one rejection define the project, and to keep pushing until he found a way through.

“You're way too young to be doing something like a non-profit. Come on, you're 10 years old. You should be in school or playing outside.”

05 · Turning Story into Action

Six pivots from idea to movement

Lecturing never worked. Candy, competition, and a good story did.

  1. 1

    Lectures fell flat

    In July 2019 he went door to door with a short pitch and battery-shaped flyers. Eyes glazed over within seconds, and most of those flyers probably went straight in the trash.

  2. 2

    Candy for batteries

    So he tried bribery. Candy for any kid who brought in used batteries. Suddenly they were showing up with bags of them.

  3. 3

    The “Battery Challenge”

    He turned recycling into a contest between classrooms, with a pizza party for the winning class. A game beat a lecture every single time.

  4. 4

    Call2Recycle says yes

    The group that first said no ended up changing its mind. He kept emailing until the CEO said yes and sent battery bins for free.

  5. 5

    COVID forces a pivot

    When COVID closed the schools, the project nearly died with them. He moved it online and kept the story alive on social media.

  6. 6

    The story goes global

    It kept spreading until it reached India, which turned a New Jersey project into a worldwide one.

06 · The Bigger Idea

He isn't counting batteries. He's changing culture.

Counting batteries was never really the point. What Sri Nihal is after is harder to measure: he wants you to go home and bug your family about it, and to hand the story to a friend the way it was once handed to him. One full battery bin is a nice afternoon. One person who changes how ten others think is a movement.

“Your age does not matter. You could be five years old, but if you have determination in your heart, you can change the world.”

Sri Nihal Tammana

Facts inform. Stories move. Recycle My Battery runs on the second one.

07 · Closing

His advice to a ten-year-old

His advice to anyone starting young is almost annoyingly simple. Your age is not the problem. Quitting after the first no is. He was ten when one story rearranged his whole life, and he has spent seven years proving that a kid with enough stubbornness can do what most adults never even try.

And if he could go back and tell his own ten-year-old self one thing, it would be this:

“Keep pushing. You are going to do great things.”

Sri Nihal Tammana
